# Plastic Waste in Small Island Developing States

The purpose of this project is to increase understanding of the deeper messages inside the data on plastic waste disposal and leakage in six Small Island Developing States (SIDS) in two ocean regions 
– the Caribbean (Saint Lucia, Grenada, and Antigua and Barbuda) and the Pacific (Fiji, Vanuatu and Samoa).


Key Findings:
1. Tourism is the most waste-intensive sector per capita — Caribbean tourists generate 6.22 kg/day (3.5× more than residents), and Pacific tourists 1.51 kg/day (10× more than Pacific households). With over 1 million visitors to Antigua & Barbuda and 894,000 to Fiji annually, this overwhelms tiny island waste systems.
2. Household & commercial sectors drive the largest total leakage volumes — Despite tourism's per-capita intensity, households contribute 64% of Caribbean leakage (1,984 tonnes) and 45% of Pacific leakage (4,294 tonnes). This is driven by incomplete collection coverage — only 56% in parts of Grenada and 35-60% in Samoa/Vanuatu.
3. "Other" plastics and PET are the most leaked polymers — Non-recyclable multi-layered packaging ("Other" category) is the #1 leaked polymer in both regions (908t Caribbean, 3,319t Pacific). PET beverage bottles are #2. PS/Styrofoam is disproportionately problematic in the Pacific (1,481t leaked vs 281t in the Caribbean).
4. Caribbean islands dispose far more waste per person than Pacific islands — 1.77 kg/person/day vs 0.14 kg/person/day, correlating with higher GDP. But the Pacific has 3× more total leakage (9,614 vs 3,087 tonnes) because 50-58% of waste in Samoa and Vanuatu is completely unmanaged.
5. Virtually zero recycling infrastructure exists — Only 59 tonnes were recycled across all three Caribbean countries (0.5% of disposal). Samoa and Vanuatu have no active plastic recyclers at all. These islands are import-dependent for plastic with no exit pathway.

Top Recommendations: 

The dashboard includes 8 evidence-based recommendations, but the highest-impact ones are implementing Container Deposit Levies (could prevent 1,000+ tonnes of PET leakage in the Pacific alone), building regional recycling hubs through OECS/SPREP coordination, and achieving universal waste collection coverage in underserved rural and peri-urban areas.
